    <section class="doc-sec" id="article">
      <h2>The article in one page</h2>
      <blockquote class="quote">
        <p>“A loop makes a unit of work correct without you. A graph decides which units exist at all.”</p>
        <cite>Hanako (@hanakoxbt)</cite>
      </blockquote>
      <div class="table-wrap "><table><thead><tr><th>Article idea</th><th>In this checkout model</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Loop: produce, check, correct, repeat until green</td><td>A PDSA cycle inside every value stream step. Study is a check a program can fail.</td></tr><tr><td>Write the check first</td><td>Human-written, locked acceptance tests (ATDD) before any code.</td></tr><tr><td>Graph: nodes and real dependencies</td><td>Contracts between the checkout team and each channel stakeholder; each edge has a promise, acceptance criteria and a signal.</td></tr><tr><td>Splitter, worker, code node, gate</td><td>Intake and slice, Copilot coding agent, deterministic pipeline, human merge approval.</td></tr><tr><td>Correction edge and learning edge</td><td>Return one failed unit to Build; send derived constraints back to Intake and Copilot instructions.</td></tr><tr><td>Gate on blast radius, not confidence</td><td>Three lanes; payment capture, ledger and card-data changes never open to agents.</td></tr><tr><td>Approve only the last step</td><td>One accountable human approves the merge and the flag ramp, not each intermediate step.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>
      <div class="callout"><strong>The one human step</strong> Put the human at the point of highest consequence and lowest reversibility: approving the merge and choosing which changes ship. A human in the middle of the graph becomes its slowest node.</div>
    </section>
